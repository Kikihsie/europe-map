Key definitions

Six groups, different purposes

EU European Union

A political and economic union whose members share institutions, a single market and a customs union. The single market allows goods, services, capital and people to move freely; the customs union applies a common external tariff to goods from outside the union.

Key distinction: EU membership does not automatically mean using the euro or belonging to Schengen.

EFTA European Free Trade Association

An intergovernmental organisation that promotes free trade and economic cooperation among Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland, and negotiates trade agreements with other countries.

Key distinction: EFTA is a free trade association, not a customs union: it has no common external tariff. Its members are outside the EU.

Schengen Schengen area

An area where routine checks on people at internal borders have been removed. Members cooperate on external border controls and short-stay visa rules. Temporary internal border checks can be reintroduced.

Key distinction: Schengen concerns border checks on people. It does not itself create a customs union or a right to work in another country.

EEA European Economic Area

An arrangement extending the EU single market to Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway. It covers the free movement of goods, services, capital and people under shared rules, with specific exclusions such as most common agricultural and fisheries policies.

Key distinction: The EEA Agreement does not include the EU customs union or monetary union. Switzerland is an EFTA member but is outside the EEA.

Eurozone Euro area

The EU member states that have adopted the euro as their currency. They share a monetary policy conducted by the Eurosystem: the European Central Bank and the national central banks of euro-area countries.

Key distinction: Some countries outside the EU also use the euro, but this does not make them members of the euro area.

Customs Union Internal tariff removal + common external tariff

A form of economic integration in which members remove tariffs on trade in covered goods between themselves and apply a common external tariff to imports from non-members.

Key distinction: Unlike a free trade area, a customs union has a common external tariff. It does not by itself guarantee free movement of workers, a single market or a shared currency.
